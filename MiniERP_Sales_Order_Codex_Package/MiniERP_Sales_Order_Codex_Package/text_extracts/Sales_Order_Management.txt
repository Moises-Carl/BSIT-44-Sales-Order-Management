1. PURPOSE AND SYSTEM SCOPE
1.1 System Overview
The Mini-ERP project consists of five interconnected systems: Product/Catalog Management, Sales/Order Management, Inventory Management, Billing/Payment Management, and Delivery/Shipment Management. Each system performs a specific business function while exchanging information with the other systems to support a complete transaction. Clear ownership of records and responsibilities is necessary to prevent duplicate data, conflicting updates, and overlapping functions.
Within this structure, the Sales/Order Management System maintains the customer’s purchase record. It identifies the customer, records the selected seller offers and quantities, preserves the accepted purchase prices, and tracks the progress of the order. It also coordinates the transfer of order information to Inventory, Billing, and Delivery at the appropriate stages of processing.
This document presents the proposed responsibilities, logical database structure, business rules, and integration requirements of the Sales/Order Management System. The design remains independent of a specific database platform because the selection between Firebase and Supabase has not yet been finalized. Shared workflow decisions must also be reviewed with the affected groups before implementation.
1.2 Purpose of the System
The purpose of the Sales/Order Management System is to provide an organized and reliable method of recording and monitoring customer orders. It maintains a consistent record of what the customer agreed to purchase, including the selected offers, quantities, prices, and recipient details. These records serve as the basis for stock reservation, billing, and fulfillment.


The system also supports coordination between the other Mini-ERP modules. It requests stock reservation from Inventory, supplies order information to Billing, and authorizes fulfillment after the required conditions have been met. Throughout this process, Sales updates the order’s overall status using verified results from the responsible systems.
An important requirement is the preservation of transaction history. A submitted order must retain the accepted purchase prices even when the seller changes the catalog prices afterward. This allows customers and authorized personnel to review an order using the details that applied when the purchase was submitted.
1.3 Functional Scope
The system covers order preparation, submission, monitoring, and eligible cancellation. Customers may prepare draft orders by selecting seller offers and entering quantities. Before submission, the system validates the required information, checks the selected offers, calculates the total amount, and presents any price changes for customer acceptance.
After submission, the system coordinates stock reservation and billing. Once payment is verified and a valid stock reservation is confirmed, Sales sends the information required for fulfillment. The system continues to receive progress updates until the order is completed or properly cancelled.

1.4 Responsibility Boundaries
Sales/Order Management owns the order records, ordered items, and order-status history. It does not directly maintain the operational records assigned to the other systems. For example, Sales may request that Inventory reserve two units, but Inventory determines whether the reservation succeeds and records the corresponding stock changes.

The same separation applies to payment and delivery information. Billing verifies payment results, while Delivery records shipment progress and delivery confirmation. Sales uses those results to update the overall order status. This arrangement allows each system to remain responsible for the accuracy of the records it maintains.

1.5 Proposed Initial Demonstration Scope
For the initial demonstration, the proposed design limits each order to products offered by one seller and uses Philippine pesos as the transaction currency. All requested items must be successfully reserved before payment is requested. Partial fulfillment and split shipments are excluded to keep the first integration manageable.
Customer cancellation is proposed before successful payment, subject to confirmation that any reservation has been released and that payment has not succeeded. Paid cancellations, returns, and refunds are outside the proposed initial scope. The order total represents the product amount only. Shipping fees, taxes, and discounts must be included only after the groups agree on their calculation and ownership. These restrictions are proposed implementation limits rather than finalized class-wide rules. The affected groups must confirm them before development proceeds.
2. DATABASE DESIGN AND ENTITY RELATIONSHIPS
2.1 Design Overview
The proposed database structure separates the main order record from its individual items and status-history entries. This allows one order to contain several products without repeating customer and recipient information for every item. It also allows status changes to be recorded separately while keeping the current status available in the main order record.
The Sales design includes Customer, Sales Order, Order Item, and Order Status History. Seller Offer is shown as an external entity because Product/Catalog Management owns it. Sales references the existing offer identifier rather than creating a separate catalog.
Customer ownership remains a shared decision. This proposal places customer contact records under Sales. If the class establishes a shared customer or account entity, Sales should reference that record and adjust the design accordingly. Customer authentication must follow the shared account arrangement rather than introducing a separate password database within Sales.

3. BUSINESS RULES
3.1 Order Creation and Submission
Every order must have a unique identifier and reference an existing customer. Draft orders may be edited, allowing customers to revise their selections before submission. Submission requires at least one valid item, positive whole-number quantities, and complete recipient details.
Selected offers must exist and remain active when the order is submitted. Under the proposed initial scope, all offers must belong to the same seller. Once submitted, the item details, accepted prices, quantities, and recipient information become locked. This prevents changes from conflicting with reservations, invoices, or fulfillment requests already created by the other systems.

3.2 Price Preservation and Amount Calculation
The system must obtain selling prices from validated Catalog information. If a price changes between item selection and submission, Sales must display the updated amount and obtain customer acceptance before continuing. After submission, the accepted price is preserved in the order item.
Line totals and order totals are calculated by the system. If these calculated amounts are stored, they must remain consistent with the quantities and accepted prices. Monetary values must use an agreed representation, such as decimal amounts with two decimal places or integer centavos.
Line Total = Quantity × Unit Price at Order
Order Total = Sum of All Line Totals
For example, an order containing two keyboards priced at ₱800.00 each has a product total of ₱1,600.00. A later catalog price of ₱850.00 must not change the submitted order.
3.3 Stock Reservation and Payment
Sales must receive complete reservation confirmation from Inventory before requesting payment. An availability check alone does not guarantee that stock has been secured. Inventory remains responsible for preventing over-reservation and maintaining accurate stock records.
If any item cannot be reserved, the order must not proceed to payment. Any partial reservation must be released through Inventory. Once all items are reserved, Billing may create the billing record and process payment. Sales may confirm the order only after Billing verifies payment and Inventory confirms that the reservation remains valid.
Payment failure may allow another attempt while the reservation remains active. If the reservation expires, fulfillment must be blocked and cancellation coordination must begin. A payment received after reservation expiry requires reconciliation before the order can continue.

3.5 Cancellation and Exception Handling
Cancellation of a submitted order requires coordination because Inventory may already hold stock and Billing may already have started payment processing. While cancellation is pending, fulfillment must remain blocked. Sales may mark the order Cancelled only after confirming that payment has not succeeded and that no active reservation remains.
If payment succeeds while cancellation is being processed, the system must hold the order for reconciliation. It must not silently cancel a paid order or authorize shipment without verifying stock. Paid cancellation and refund procedures remain outside the proposed first demonstration.

3.6 Access Control and Record Retention
Customers may create orders, review their own records, edit their drafts, and request eligible cancellation. Authorized staff may perform actions permitted by their assigned role. Connected systems may submit results only for the functions they own. Neither customers nor sales staff may manually bypass stock or payment verification.
Submitted orders, item records, and status history must remain available for review. Ordinary user actions must not permanently delete these records because they may be referenced by Inventory, Billing, or Delivery.

4. INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS
4.1 Integration Approach
The Sales/Order Management System exchanges information with all four other systems. Each exchange must identify the relevant order and provide enough information for the receiving system to perform its responsibility. The implementation may use shared database operations, APIs, or events, provided that ownership and validation rules remain consistent.
Order IDs must be shared across the connected records. Item-level responses must identify the affected order items, while reservation, billing, and fulfillment requests must include unique request references. Repeated requests must be recognized safely to prevent duplicate processing.
4.2 Product/Catalog Management
Sales receives offer identifiers, product names, seller references, current prices, and offer status from Catalog. It uses this information during item selection and submission. Catalog remains responsible for the master product and offer records, while Sales preserves the purchase details accepted for each submitted transaction.
4.3 Inventory Management
Sales sends the order ID, item IDs, selected offer IDs, and requested quantities to Inventory. Inventory returns reservation results, reservation references, expiry information, and release confirmations. The groups must agree on how each seller offer maps to the correct inventory record.
Inventory alone performs stock reservation, release, deduction, and movement recording. Sales must wait for a definite result before advancing the order.
4.4 Billing/Payment Management
After successful reservation, Sales provides Billing with the order ID, customer or billing details, item quantities, accepted prices, and total product amount. Billing returns invoice references, payment status, verified amounts, and transaction results.
The proposed demonstration requires full verified payment before fulfillment. Payment failure, late payment, or conflicting results must be reported clearly so Sales can preserve a consistent order state.
4.5 Delivery/Shipment Management
Sales sends the order reference, item details, recipient information, requested address, and fulfillment authorization to Delivery after payment and reservation checks succeed. Delivery returns shipment acceptance, shipment references, progress updates, delivery confirmation, and failure information.
Sales records the overall order as Fulfilling after acceptance and Completed after verified delivery, provided payment remains confirmed. Delivery continues to own courier assignments, tracking details, and shipment records.

4.7 Shared Processing Requirements
All groups must agree on identifier formats, field names, status values, timestamp conventions, and monetary precision. Responses must clearly indicate whether processing succeeded, failed, or remains pending. A timeout must not automatically be interpreted as success.
Each system must recognize repeated requests and validate updates against the current state. When information is missing or contradictory, the order must remain unresolved until the responsible systems reconcile the result.
5. PROPOSED END-TO-END WORKFLOW
The process begins when a customer prepares a draft order. Sales validates the selected offers, quantities, and recipient details. If prices have changed, the customer reviews the revised total before submission. Sales then preserves the accepted details and requests reservation from Inventory.
After Inventory confirms that all items are reserved, Sales submits the order to Billing. Billing processes payment and communicates the verified result. Sales confirms the order only when payment is successful and the reservation remains valid. It then sends a fulfillment request to Delivery.
Delivery accepts the request and provides shipment updates. Sales displays the order’s progress and records relevant status changes. Once Delivery confirms successful delivery and payment remains verified, Sales marks the order Completed.
When a transaction cannot proceed, Sales follows the applicable exception or cancellation process. This ensures that unresolved stock, payment, or delivery information does not produce an incorrect completed or cancelled order.
