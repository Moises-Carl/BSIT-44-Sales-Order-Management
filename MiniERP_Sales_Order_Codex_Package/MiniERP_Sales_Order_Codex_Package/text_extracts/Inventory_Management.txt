1. PURPOSE AND SYSTEM SCOPE

1.1 System Overview

       The Mini-ERP project divides the operations of an online marketplace into five
connected systems: Product/Catalog Management, Sales/Order Management, Inventory
Management, Billing/Payment Management, and Delivery/Shipment Management. A
single customer purchase passes through all five, so each system must know exactly which
records it controls and which records it only reads from the others.

       The Inventory Management System is the point of truth for stock. Whenever
another system needs to know whether a seller can still sell a product, or needs units set
aside for a customer, the request comes to Inventory. Inventory answers using its own
counts and records every change it makes, so that each stock figure can be traced to the
events that produced it.

       This document describes the responsibilities, logical database structure, business
rules, and integration requirements proposed by the Inventory Management group. The
design is written without depending on a particular database platform, since the class has
not yet chosen between Firebase and Supabase. Items that affect other groups are listed in
Section 6 for joint confirmation.

1.2 Purpose of the System

       The main purpose of the Inventory Management System is to keep stock figures
accurate while orders are being placed, paid for, and shipped. It does this by separating the
units a seller physically holds from the units already promised to customers. The difference
between the two is the quantity that can still be sold.

       The system also protects the marketplace from overselling. When two customers
attempt to buy the last available unit at the same moment, only one reservation may
succeed. Reservation, release, and deduction are therefore handled only by Inventory and
are processed one at a time for each stock record.

adds stock back when a returned package is received.

1.4 Responsibility Boundaries

       Inventory owns the stock records, reservations, and movement log. It does not store
product descriptions, prices, order contents, payments, or shipment details. Where it needs
to connect with those records, it keeps only the identifier used by the owning system. For
example, a reservation stores the order item identifier from Sales, but the ordered quantity
and price shown to the customer remain in the Sales record.

       The same rule works in reverse. Sales may ask Inventory to reserve three units, but
only Inventory decides whether the request succeeds and changes the stock figures.
Delivery may report that a package left the seller, but only Inventory deducts the units and
writes the movement entry. Table 2 shows how record ownership is divided among the five

1.5 Proposed Initial Demonstration Scope

       For the first demonstration, each seller offer has a single stock record and no
separate warehouse locations. Stock is counted in whole units. A reservation request for an
order is processed as one unit of work: either every item is reserved or none of them is, so
the system never leaves a partial reservation behind.

       Unpaid reservations are proposed to expire after 30 minutes. Stock is deducted at
dispatch rather than at payment, so that a cancellation before dispatch only needs a release.
Supplier purchasing, multiple warehouses, batch or expiry-date tracking, and stock
transfers between sellers are outside the initial scope. These limits are proposals and must
be confirmed with the affected groups.

3. BUSINESS RULES

3.1 Stock Record Rules

        A stock record may be created only for a seller offer that exists in Product/Catalog
Management. Each offer has at most one stock record. Quantities are whole numbers and
may never fall below zero, and the reserved quantity may never exceed the quantity on
hand.

        The quantity on hand may change only through a recorded movement, and the
movement must be saved in the same database transaction as the new balance. Stock
records that already have movement history may not be deleted, because reservations and
log entries depend on them. These requirements are listed in Table 9.

3.2 Available Stock Calculation

        The quantity that customers may still buy is computed from the two stored figures
and is never saved as a separate value. Low-stock and out-of-stock conditions are derived
from the same result.

             Available Quantity = Quantity on Hand − Quantity Reserved

                    Low Stock: Available Quantity ≤ Reorder Level

                           Out of Stock: Available Quantity = 0

        For example, a seller holds 15 wireless mice with a reorder level of 5. Two pending
orders hold 4 and 3 units, so the available quantity is 15 − 7 = 8. If a third order reserves 3
more, the available quantity becomes 5 and the record is reported as low stock, even though
15 units are still physically present.

3.3 Reservation Rules

        A reservation request from Sales lists every item of one order. Inventory checks all
items before changing anything. If any item lacks enough available stock, or its seller offer
is no longer active, the whole request is rejected and the response identifies the affected
items. If every item passes, all reservations are created together.

3.5 Stock Movement Rules

        Each movement type moves stock in one direction. Restock, Return, and
Adjustment In increase the quantity on hand. Sale and Adjustment Out decrease it.
Adjustments require remarks, and an Adjustment Out may not reduce the quantity on hand
below the quantity reserved, because those units are already promised to customers.

        Movements are never changed after they are saved. If a restock was entered with
the wrong quantity, the seller records an adjustment for the difference, leaving both entries
visible in the history.

4. INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS

4.1 Integration Approach

       Inventory exchanges information with Product/Catalog, Sales/Order, and
Delivery/Shipment Management. It does not exchange information with Billing/Payment
Management directly: payment results reach Inventory through Sales, which coordinates
the order. This keeps one system responsible for deciding when an order moves forward.

       Every request must state the identifiers Inventory needs and a unique reference, and
every response must clearly report success, failure, or a pending result. A request that times
out must not be treated as successful. The sender should repeat it with the same reference
so that Inventory can return the original outcome.

4.2 Product/Catalog Management

       Inventory receives seller offer identifiers and the status of each offer, product, and
seller from Catalog. It uses them when a stock record is created and before any new
reservation. In return, Inventory provides the available quantity and low-stock condition
for each offer, which Catalog may display on product pages.

4.3 Sales/Order Management

       Sales sends reservation requests containing the order identifier, each order item
identifier with its seller offer and quantity, and a request reference. Inventory replies with
                                                                                                 18
                                                                                                 Page




                         COLLEGE OF COMPUTING STUDIES

a single result for the order, the reservation identifiers, and the expiry time, or with the list
of items that could not be reserved.

        Sales also notifies Inventory when payment is verified, so that the reservations
become Confirmed, and when an order is cancelled or payment fails, so that the
reservations are released. The groups must agree on how the offer identifier used by Sales
maps to the seller and product pair used by Inventory.

4.4 Billing/Payment Management

        No direct exchange is proposed. Billing reports payment results to Sales, and Sales
forwards the effect on stock to Inventory. If the class later decides that Billing should notify
Inventory directly, the same confirmation and release functions can be reused without
changing the database design.

4.5 Delivery/Shipment Management

        Delivery may read the reservation status of an order to confirm that its stock is still
held before preparing a shipment. When the package is dispatched, Delivery sends the
order identifier, the order item identifiers, and a source event reference. Inventory then
marks the reservations Fulfilled, deducts the units, and records Sale movements.

        When a failed or returned delivery reaches the seller, Delivery sends a return
notification with its own event reference. Inventory records Return movements and adds
the units back to the quantity on hand.

4.7 Shared Processing Requirements

        All groups must use the same identifier formats, status names, and timestamp
conventions. Inventory requires a unique reference on every request and event it receives,
and it rejects updates that do not fit the current reservation status, such as a dispatch notice
for a released reservation.

        When a result is missing or contradictory, the affected reservation stays in its
current status until the responsible systems resolve the difference. Inventory never guesses
a result in order to move a reservation forward.

5. PROPOSED END-TO-END WORKFLOW

       The process begins when a seller records stock for an active seller offer. Inventory
creates the stock record and logs a Restock movement. From that point, the offer’s
available quantity can be shown to customers.

       When a customer submits an order, Sales sends a reservation request. Inventory
checks every item, locks the affected stock records, and either reserves all items with a 30-
minute expiry or rejects the request with the list of short items. The quantity on hand does
not change at this stage.

       After Billing verifies payment, Sales notifies Inventory and the reservations
become Confirmed. If payment fails or the customer cancels, Sales requests a release. If
no notice arrives before the expiry time, Inventory expires the reservations on its own. In
each of these cases, the units return to available stock.

       When Delivery dispatches the package, Inventory marks the reservations Fulfilled,
reduces the quantity on hand and the quantity reserved, and records Sale movements. If the
package later returns to the seller, Inventory records Return movements. Throughout the
process, every change to the physical count leaves a permanent entry in the movement log.
